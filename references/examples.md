# Calibration examples

These are invented teaching examples, not quotations from Krumpy, facts about his projects, or preferred wording to reuse. Each preserves the supplied substance. The goal is judgement that transfers to other topics.

## Proposal: mechanism before praise

Draft:
> This transformative booking solution will seamlessly empower residents. We propose opening unclaimed appointment slots 24 hours in advance. It could reduce empty appointments, although we have not tested whether residents can attend at that notice.

Edited:
> We propose opening unclaimed appointment slots 24 hours in advance. This could reduce empty appointments, but we still need to test whether residents can attend at that notice.

The proposal and uncertainty remain. No invented success metric or user research was added. If “we” is ambiguous in the document, identify the proposing team from the source or flag the missing actor.

## Scope: preserve the tradeoff

Draft:
> The current process allows requests to sit with no named owner. The proposal is to assign each request to a coordinator at submission. This makes ownership clear, but it also means the coordinator needs a way to transfer work when they are away. The cover arrangement is TBD.

Edited:
> Requests can currently sit without a named owner. Assigning each request to a coordinator at submission would make ownership clear, but coordinators also need a way to transfer work when they are away. The cover arrangement is still to be decided.

This is a proposal, not an implemented workflow. The unresolved detail is retained rather than filled with an invented rota.

## Candid discussion: keep the actual position

Draft:
> I think the registration form asks too much of people before they've even decided whether to join. We need their contact details, but I'm not convinced we need their full work history at this stage. I'd like to trial a shorter form before we rebuild the whole thing.

Edited:
> I think the registration form asks too much of people before they've decided whether to join. We need their contact details, but I'm not convinced we need their full work history at this stage. I'd like to trial a shorter form before rebuilding the whole thing.

A light edit is enough. Do not add jokes, turn the view into consensus, or strip every “I think” as a hedge.

## Documentation: exact terms survive

Draft:
> The export includes three fields: account_id, created_at and status. Deleted accounts are excluded. Exports may take up to ten minutes. The surface area is recorded in square metres.

Edited: unchanged.

The three-item list is factual, the passive voice is clear, the uncertainty is functional, and “surface” has a literal meaning. None needs repair.

## Review-only: no unsolicited rewrite

Request: “Use $krumpy-writing to audit this: ‘This initiative will revolutionise onboarding. A pilot has not yet been scheduled.’”

Response:
> “Will revolutionise onboarding” promises an outcome that the unscheduled pilot does not establish. Describe the intended improvement, or label it as an expectation if the supporting brief gives one.

Do not rewrite the whole passage, invent what the improvement is, or silently edit its file.

## Conflicting source: disclose it

Source notes: “The trial lasts six weeks.” Later: “Review results after the eight-week trial.”

A faithful rewrite cannot quietly choose a duration. Ask which is correct if the final wording depends on it, or use an explicit unresolved-duration note in a working draft. Do not say the pilot lasts “six to eight weeks”; the source never established a range.

## Domain transfer

A school transport proposal should discuss the relevant journey, cost, people, conditions and consequences. It should not import players, guilds, game economies or a past personal anecdote simply because those appeared in the style samples.

## New draft from a short brief

Brief: “Propose a weekly 20-minute handover for the support team. Cases are being passed on without context. Ownership should be named. We haven't chosen a start date.”

Draft:
> Cases are being passed between support staff without enough context. I propose a weekly 20-minute handover so the team can explain what has happened on each open case and name the person responsible for the next action. The start date is still to be agreed.

The first person expresses the proposal the user actually supplied. It does not invent a trial, savings estimate or approval.
