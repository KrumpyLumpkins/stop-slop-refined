# Match the document to its reader

Choose the form from the current request and destination. These are useful shapes, not templates to impose.

## Proposal or scope

Make the problem and proposed decision visible early. Explain the mechanism before claiming benefits. Separate requirements from optional ideas, alternatives, assumptions and unresolved parameters. A Problem / Proposal / Requirements / Expected result structure fits many scopes. A funding or governance proposal may instead need the actual ask, budget, ownership, delivery conditions and uncertainties.

Use a worked example when it helps the reader assess a rule. Preserve who is affected and what they give up. Do not make an unresolved budget, date, owner or approval sound settled. Use the required organisational template when supplied. Keep questions that merely improve background context outside the proposal when the user requests a clean circulation draft; retain unresolved information inside it where readers need it to assess the decision.

## Documentation or instructions

Organise around what the reader needs to understand or do. State prerequisites, steps, results and exceptions where relevant. Distinguish current behaviour from future plans. Explain an unfamiliar term once and use it consistently. Keep values, code, formulas and identifiers exact. A precise passive sentence or repeated noun can be the clearest choice.

For reader review, ask: Can someone use this without the original chat? What would they likely misunderstand? Does an example imply broader support than the rule permits?

## Essay or discussion note

Keep the author's actual view and chain of reasoning. Allow uncertainty and credible counterarguments. Let examples illuminate the point instead of replacing it. A conversational question belongs when it opens a real issue, not when it manufactures suspense. Do not force headings or bullet summaries into a piece that reads better as connected prose.

## Email, message or announcement

Lead with the purpose. Include the relevant context, concrete update or request, and any real next action. Match the relationship and channel. Avoid formalising a simple message into a proposal, or making an important request so terse it loses its reason.

## Neutral editing or another person's writing

When requested, use the anti-slop and fidelity checks without the personal voice overlay. Preserve the existing author's useful register. Review someone else's text as text; do not attribute its wording to Krumpy or diagnose who wrote it.
